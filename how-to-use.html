How To Use This Tool

Tips on choosing locations, understanding Spike Scores, and getting the most out of your time outdoors.

Back to Tool

What is the "What to Watch For" tool?

This tool uses the iNaturalist platform and looks at all the organisms that have been reported in the locations you select. It then looks at when those organisms are observed and sorts them to show species that are more unique to that timeframe.

What is this tool meant for?

The What to Watch For tool is meant to guide and inspire your time out in nature. I am hopeful that this tool can give parents and teachers a starting point to help kids find new and interesting things in the natural spaces around them all year long. This tool is also great for anyone who would like a sneak peek at what they might find in nature when they are out.

What locations should I choose?

This really depends on your goals. Generally speaking, I have found that broader locations give better results for the spirit of the tool:

  • Local level: Choosing a few adjoining counties will give you a good gauge of what is being seen locally.
  • Regional level: Choosing a state or multiple states will give you a good gauge of what is being seen regionally.
  • Hyper-local level: Choosing a hyper-local location like a single park will not include observations outside of that small area. This is great if you want to know what is being seen in that specific park, but will not be likely to guide you anywhere else.

Example: A broad location like "Minnesota" will include smaller locations within it like "Olmsted County" and "Quarry Hill Nature Center", but a smaller location like "Quarry Hill Nature Center" will not include observed species outside of that park.

How many locations can I choose?

You can select up to 3 locations. I suggest picking locations that have a similar environment. For example, Minnesota and Wisconsin together would give more useful results than Minnesota and Florida.

What are the options for locations?

Anywhere that is a registered location in iNaturalist can be used. This includes almost all counties, states, and countries. Some cities and parks are also locations in iNaturalist, but not all. iNaturalist is a crowd-sourced community and most of the locations have been user-submitted.

What does the Spike Score mean?

The Spike Score is the percentage of all observations of that species that fall in the time window you selected. The higher the Spike Score, the more unique that species is to that time of year.

Example: If a species has a Spike Score of 51.2%, it means that 51.2% of all observations of that species in that location happened in the selected timeframe.

Where does the data come from?

The data is sourced from iNaturalist using their API and eBird using the GBIF database.
